Cadence 2.0.18 - Quick guide

The key examples use the original QWERTY profile. The list at the end shows the active shortcuts for your layout, including your custom bindings.

----------------------------------------
Open, save and export

File > Open media appends video, WAV or MP3 files in sequence. You can also drop files into the window or double-click the empty welcome screen. An audio-only file has a black picture. The first file sets the timeline frame rate.

Ctrl+S saves the edit, drawings and comparison references to a .cadence project. Keep the media files in their original locations. Save as creates another project. Ctrl+N starts an empty edit.

Ctrl+E opens the export dialog. MP4 and MOV H.264 are suitable for review. For editorial, choose MOV ProRes 422 HQ, ProRes 4444 with alpha, or DNxHR HQ. These profiles preserve the timeline frame rate and mix with 48 kHz stereo PCM. WAV and MP3 export audio only. GIFs are silent and can recover a deleted source from its open file or cached preview.

PNG, RLE Targa and OpenEXR sequences create a folder of numbered images, for example shot-png/shot.1001.png. Choose the starting frame number. “Drawings only” preserves transparency and drawing holds in sequences and ProRes 4444. PNG, Targa and ProRes 4444 use straight alpha. ZIP16 half-float OpenEXR converts the Cadence sRGB review render to premultiplied linear RGB, with the precision of the original review render. “Include drawings in export” adds drawings and active onion skins to the edit.

----------------------------------------
Playback, viewing and panels

Space plays or pauses. Left and Right step one frame in either direction. Comma and Period jump between drawing keys and markers. K switches those two shortcuts between keys and frames. The Previous keyframe and Next keyframe buttons always visit drawing keys and markers. The loop button remembers your playback choice. Up and Down, or Ctrl + wheel, change the volume in 5% steps. In a list or menu, the arrow keys navigate.

Scrub by dragging on the timeline, on empty space in its header or the playback controls, or on the video with the Pointer tool. Hold Shift on the video for finer scrubbing. Shift-drag on the timeline selects a range and displays its duration. Release the mouse before Shift to keep the range. I and O set its bounds. Escape clears the selection.

In both Playback and Editing modes, the header lets you add a track, open media, save and export. In Playback mode, hover over the top of the video to reveal it. Hover over the strip just above the ruler to reveal the lower controls. Editing mode initially shows every panel. View > Playback / editing mode settings customizes each workspace. F10 opens the main menu when the key is available.

Drag dividers to resize panels. View > Edit panel layout allows moving or detaching them. Reset panel layout restores the current mode. File > Options > Preferences > Restore interface resets both modes.

Use the wheel to zoom the video. Middle-drag to pan, Ctrl + horizontal middle-drag to zoom and Shift + vertical middle-drag to rotate. 0 resets the view and its flips. F11 or a double-click enters full screen. Escape exits it. View zoom, pan and rotation affect the preview. Flips (H and Shift+V) affect exports. Click Exposure to switch the selected tracks between their correction and 0 EV. The displayed setting is used for export.

The timeline shows frame numbers by default. The timecode overlay position is locked by default. Right-click the overlay to unlock and move it. View > Duplicate video view opens an independent, synchronized window, also available by right-clicking the video. Drag the picture to move the window or its edges to resize it. Hover near the lower center to access playback, always-on-top and opacity controls. Space plays or pauses, Tab reveals the controls and Escape closes the window.

----------------------------------------
Library, media and references

Library stores free playlists and watched folders. New… creates an empty playlist, creates one from files or watches a folder. Click a folder to list its immediate videos. Double-click to open those videos in sequence, or to expand or collapse its subfolders when it has no immediate videos.

Click a video to select it, Ctrl-click to add or remove one and Shift-click to select a range. Double-click to play the chosen video. With several videos selected, double-click one of them to append them to the active track in list order and play them. Delete removes selected files or folders from the library and keeps their originals on disk. Refresh the parent folder to show removed subfolders again. Opening a playlist replaces the timeline and stores the previous edit in Temporary, including tracks, drawings, markers and undo history. Save temporary edits you want to keep after closing Cadence.

Add videos or drop them onto a free playlist to keep their paths. A watched folder copies external videos and adds a suffix for duplicate filenames. Drag to reorder. Right-click to rename any list, including a subfolder, while preserving its folder name on disk. Remove from playlist keeps the file. Refresh scans the folder again. Move to Recycle Bin asks for confirmation. Replace file path locates a moved video for that list.

In Media, right-click to append sources to a track. Drop them onto the track to choose a position. From Library you can also drop videos, lists or folders, including their subfolders. The insertion line shows the clip edge or the target position in a gap. In References, use the arrows or drag to reorder B sources.

----------------------------------------
Edit the timeline

Each media track holds video and audio. Add a second track with “+ Media track”. Drag track titles to reorder them. The upper track appears in front of the lower one and both contribute to the audio mix. Click a title to select a track. Ctrl-click its title or lane to add or remove that track. At least one track stays selected. Edits apply to the selected tracks.

Double-click a track title to expand or collapse its audio. Double-click a clip to select its duration. Shift + double-click selects the whole track. Ctrl + double-click adds or removes one clip. Ctrl + Shift + double-click extends the selection from the last chosen clip through the target, inclusive. With no prior selection, it starts at the playhead. Playback and editing respect discontinuous selections.

Hover over a clip edge to see its first or last frame. Drag that edge to hide or recover frames, even between adjoining clips. When you drag the left edge inward, the gap stays visible until you release it, then the clips close up. The Editing menu in the viewer header holds the link and copy options. Use Drawing or Comments below the colour swatch to open the corresponding panel. Click the active button again to close it.

When audio and video are unlinked, the longer stream continues on its own. Projects saved with different audio and video lengths on the same track require Cadence 2.0.16 or a compatible version to reopen.

Right-click a clip and select “Reverse playback” to play its frames backwards in the same timeline slot. Its linked audio follows. Select the command again to restore forward playback.

In the expanded timeline header, Time Warp opens the curve for the selected clip or the clip under the playhead on the active track. The horizontal axis shows timeline frames and the vertical axis shows source frames. Double-click to add a point, then drag it. A falling slope reverses motion and a horizontal segment holds a pose. The interpolation menu applies to the interval under the cursor. A/B displays the original beside the result. Apply keeps the clip duration and audio timing. Ctrl+Z undoes the change. Projects containing a curve require Cadence 2.0.17 or a compatible version.

RIFE uses Vulkan. To choose a local installation of RIFE or NVIDIA NvOFFRUC, select the engine and click “…”. For NVIDIA, choose NVEncC64.exe in a folder containing the NvOFFRUC libraries for your GPU. The project saves the curve. Cadence can rebuild the frame cache from the source media using the same engine.

Hold Alt throughout a drag to move a clip or range. Add Shift to copy. In detailed view, drop on the destination track. In simplified view, drop on the track title at the left to move between tracks. Moving between tracks closes the gap on the source track. Escape cancels the drag. On the same track, “Leave a gap” preserves the original space. Dropping into a gap fills the gap before shifting later clips.

On the timeline, Ctrl+C copies and Ctrl+X cuts the selection, including clips selected across separate ranges. With no selection, either command takes the current frame. Ctrl+V pastes the latest copy at the playhead. Delete removes the range. “Copy/paste audio” and the audio/video and drawings/video link options determine what follows the edit. Drawings and markers are shared across tracks. Linked drawings follow edits when both tracks are selected.

Right-click during a drag or drawing stroke to cancel the gesture. Otherwise, right-click a clip to split, duplicate or disable it, remove only its video or audio, or remove the entire clip and close the gap. Right-click a track title to mute the entire track, including in exports, or to reorder or remove it. Ctrl+Z undoes the edit.

The wheel zooms the timeline. Ctrl + middle-drag or the scrollbar pans horizontally. Ctrl+0 fits the timeline. Use the context menu to switch between time and frame units or simplified and detailed views. In simplified view, markers sit on the media strip and drawing keys at the bottom of the ruler.

Right-click a clip and choose Change frame rate to repeat or skip frames while preserving duration, or change speed with matching audio. Pitch is preserved by default. The timeline frame rate stays fixed. Linked drawings follow, and you can undo the change.

----------------------------------------
Draw and annotate

Choose a tool from the toolbar or Tools menu. Q returns to Pointer. Drawing applies to the current frame or selected range. Adjust color, opacity, size and pressure sensitivity. Shift + horizontal drag adjusts size before drawing. P activates the eyedropper. Ctrl-click with a drawing tool to sample a color.

Once a shape is started, Shift constrains proportions or angle, Ctrl draws from the center, Alt moves and Alt+Ctrl rotates. With Text, draw a box and type inside it. Enter or clicking outside applies the text, Shift+Enter adds a line and Escape cancels. Double-click text to edit it.

In the viewer, the lasso selects and moves objects. Ctrl+C copies and Ctrl+X cuts those objects. Ctrl+V pastes them onto the key at the playhead. Delete removes selected objects. Color or opacity changes apply across the key. T moves all objects on the current key. Ctrl+T transforms the selection or the clip. Alt+C crops the clip. Drag a handle to resize or drag outside a corner to rotate. Shift preserves proportions or snaps rotation to 15°, Ctrl uses the center and Alt moves. Enter applies, Escape cancels. Clip framing affects exports.

Drag a drawing key to move it or its handles to change its exposure. Shift-drag selects several keys. Alt-drag moves a selected key range. C copies, X cuts, V pastes, D deletes and Shift+K duplicates at the playhead. For keyboard adjustments, Tab to the timeline and press Enter to focus a key. Shift+Tab and Tab move from the key body to its handles. Left or Right moves the focused part one frame. Enter or Escape leaves this adjustment.

S adds or removes a marker at the current frame. With a timeline selection, S or the + Marker button places a marker spanning each selected range. Hover over a flag to read its comment and scroll longer text with the wheel. This option is in Preferences > Interface. Drag its flag to move it. Right-click > Rename marker… changes its name. An empty name displays just the flag. Show drawing layer controls visibility. Gamma applies an overlay beneath the drawings, from solid black at -10 to solid white at +10. At 0 the original colours return. Click Gamma to switch the overlay off or back on. The displayed setting is used for export. Onion skins extend drawings before or after each key with decreasing opacity. A range of zero disables that side.

----------------------------------------
Compare A and B

A is the edit. Choose References > Add B references to load one or more videos. You can also right-click a track title to use that track as B and the other as A in the preview. The export keeps the complete edit and audio mix.

A/B toggles comparison. If no B source is loaded, enabling comparison opens the References tab. Ctrl+9 opens this tab at any time. Choose the B source, offset and display mode in the same place. Overlay has an opacity control and Difference has a gain control. Shift+X cycles through the modes and Alt+X swaps A and B.

Opacity adjusts Overlay, Gain amplifies differences and Reference offset aligns B in timeline frames. Drag the Wipe divider. For keyboard control, click the handle or Tab to the video and press Enter. Left and Right move the divider by 2%. Enter or Tab commits. Escape restores its initial position.

----------------------------------------
Synchronize with Blender or Maya

The synchronization button beside A/B opens the 3D settings. Enable the link, choose the host and restart it after the first connector installation. If Maya asks for permission to run its startup script, check the Cadence connector before allowing it. Synchronized instance identifies the process by version, scene and PID. A disconnection puts the link on hold. Select another instance to change the target.

Choose the direction of synchronization. By default, the start of the 3D range is frame 0 in Cadence. An offset of +10 maps it to frame 10. Cadence converts positions between frame rates and rounds to the nearest whole frame in Maya. Match frame rate adapts the current media or all media while preserving duration and can be undone. Synchronization starts disabled.

In a studio, restrict write access to Maya and Blender script directories to the artist and the administrators who maintain the tools.

In the 3D settings, choose a camera and Send drawings. Sending is manual and replaces the previous transfer from the same project to that camera, including its touch-ups. Timing follows the frame rate and offset. A key shorter than one 3D frame can disappear. Include onion skins uses the active Cadence settings.

Blender 4.3 or later groups drawings by project and camera in the “Cadence Drawings” collection. Grease Pencil creates editable strokes for the brush and simple shapes. A key containing text, an eraser, a group or a non-uniform transform is sent as a transparent image. Images are packed into the .blend on save. Solid views switch to Texture color to show them.

Maya 2022 supports editable native Grease Pencil frames. Cancel in Cadence or Escape in Maya stops the transfer between steps and keeps the previous drawings. The “Cadence_Drawings” display layer groups those frames and transparent image planes. Maya 2023 or later uses a Blue Pencil layer for viewport display and playblasts. Text and shapes are flattened. Transparent images creates an animated plane. Keep sourceimages/Cadence with the scene. Resend images after changing Maya’s view transform.

Transfer limits are 4,096 pixels on the longest side, and 25,000 image changes and 2 GB for Blue Pencil and image sequences. Remove drawings asks for confirmation, then removes all Cadence transfers in the scene across projects and cameras. The user’s native drawings and image files on disk are preserved.

----------------------------------------
Capture, preferences and updates

File > Capture screen selects the screen or region, MP4 or GIF, frame rate, cursor, key and click overlays, system audio and microphone. A countdown precedes recording. On Windows, Ctrl+Shift+F8 is the fixed global recording shortcut, even if you rebind the command inside Cadence. It also works from another application when that combination is available. Captures without a destination folder are temporary. Move those you want to keep.

File > Options contains preferences, shortcuts and navigation gestures. Preview resolution ranges from native to 4K and is independent of export resolution. HDR playback is unsupported. Video renderer changes take effect on restart. FR / en switches language immediately.

QWERTY is the default shortcut layout. When first switching to AZERTY, bindings follow the physical key positions. Each profile then keeps its settings. Click a shortcut field to reassign the key. Use Import, Export and Reset to manage your settings. Video scrubbing defaults to 25 pixels per frame, with tooltips off. Drag horizontally in a numeric field or hold Shift for finer control.

Cadence checks for Windows 2.0 updates at startup and daily while the application stays open. Previews are included by default. Both settings are in Preferences. Use Help > Check for updates for an immediate check, even when running Cadence as administrator. Portable and per-user copies download and verify the update in the background. Choose Restart now or Later. On restart, Cadence replaces the executable at its original location and keeps file associations. If the new version fails to start, Cadence restores the previous version. For a shared installation in Program Files\Cadence 2.0, enable maintenance during setup or choose Help > Check for updates > Enable updates… in the installed application. Enabling maintenance requires administrator rights. Users then receive the same restart prompt. After the update, Cadence reopens with the usual permissions of your Windows session. Close other running copies of Cadence before applying the update.

Review comments
Open Comments in the right panel. Click a marker or use ↑ and ↓ in the list to jump to its frame. Delete or the context menu removes the marker and selects the next one while keeping the playhead in place. If ranges overlap, the panel shows the most recently started active marker.

You can write a comment before creating its marker. Select a range in the timeline or enter one in the panel, then press Enter to confirm. Right-click Frame or Duration (frames) to switch to Frame ranges. Enter 52-320 to include both endpoints, or 5, 8, 10-20, 9 to place the comment at several positions. Right-click a marker title in Comments to rename it. Hover over a marker to tick its checkboxes or select text and copy it with Ctrl+C.

After a video or audio export, Cadence offers to save the edit’s markers as CSV or OpenTimelineIO in the output folder. The OpenTimelineIO file links directly to the exported media.

Cadence CSV preserves text, name, duration and frame rate. In Keyframe Pro 2, import it as CSV notes using the Frame and Notes columns (first frame = 1). Cadence also reads CSV notes from SyncSketch and xSTUDIO. Enter the first source frame or starting timecode and its position in the edit. For xSTUDIO, export notes for the media you are matching.

For Open RV, export OpenTimelineIO markers and choose the destination folder. In the Save dialog, use Browse beside the Video field to select the rendered review movie. The .otio references that movie. Keep it accessible to the recipient. Cadence imports OTIO video track and clip markers from linear edits, accounting for cuts and frame rates.

Help > Log folder opens the diagnostics to attach to a report.
